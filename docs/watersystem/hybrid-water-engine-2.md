Confirmed cinematic gaps: the CPU immersion equation omitted the GPU's frozen
motion envelope, its wake call converted the absolute tick to float, and the
shoaling envelope had slope discontinuities at three and sixteen metres.
Camera-relative impulse selection also needs to be shared by off-camera spray
sampling instead of selecting another set of eight impulses.

The next disturbance trace confirmed that entry capture checked the existing
96-block presentation range but the post-tick entry path did not, and the wake
handler sampled SPH before any distance admission. Distant effects could enter
the capped pools and evict nearby ones. `RippleRenderer` also aged absolute
ticks as floats, losing fractional expansion in old worlds.

## First checkpoint design

Reuse the current shader and `DepthWaveResponse`. Ease the two depth bands with
cubic smoothstep, keeping the existing dry/deep endpoints and 1.2 peak scale.
Carrier headings, frequencies, world coordinates, and phase clocks do not
change. Mirror the arithmetic in the active vertex shader.

Extend `WaterSurfaceEquation` compatibly with a frozen-fraction overload.
Attenuate waves and impulses by the same `1 - 0.94 * frozenFraction` used on the
GPU, after continuity taper. Tide stays outside this cosmetic envelope. Old
callers retain unfrozen behavior. Client immersion consumes the same immutable
render-frame frozen fraction as shader upload and selects wakes from the render
camera with a double-precision tick. This does not change fluid occupancy,
regional liquid/ice inventory, server physics ownership, or canonical volume.

Add deterministic endpoint/slope, freeze/tide, and production-vertex GPU
regressions. A hidden OpenGL transform-feedback fixture should compare actual
shader output with CPU heights over calm/storm spectra, frozen states, and
shoreline continuity. Such a fixture proves sampled shader arithmetic on the
tested driver, not Minecraft visuals or full-world seams.

No new dependencies, config keys, payloads, simulation regions, or persistent
data are needed. Existing configuration defaults and public overloads remain.

## Bounded disturbance follow-up

`WaterSurfaceDisplacement.withinEffectRange` now owns the existing 96-block
cosmetic range check. Both entry tick phases and `ClientWaveEntityEffects`
apply it before their water/SPH/effect work; they also reject an absent local
player or mismatched client level. Out-of-range boat presentation is released.
This policy gates **only client presentation**. Canonical transfers, regional
hydrology, server entity forces, and distant dams are not distance-paused.

`RippleRenderer` reuses the exact frame clock and computes its bounded life
fraction after subtracting the integer birth tick in double precision. Ripple,
wake and GPU impulse ages therefore retain partial ticks in long-running
worlds. The existing ring/displacement pools, caps, cadence, coordinator draw
stage, and unload cleanup remain the owners. No new region or simulation
manager was added. Range/lifetime regressions accompany this follow-up; a live
high-entity-count stress test remains unrun.

## Remaining acceptance and compatibility limits

1. No physical reactive-region solver, canonical flux bridge, pressure-driven
   floodgate adapter, or physical surge was added in the cinematic checkpoint.
   The later canonical-voxel dam fixture and initial opt-in physical shallow
   pools are documented separately above. General physical regions remain
   incomplete; the existing finite voxel solver remains active.
2. Real-client calm/storm appearance, immersion during freezing, high-disturbance
   stress, resource reload, external shaders, different graphics presets,
   historical worlds, two clients and long-duration performance remain unrun.
3. Actual GPU comparisons use known encoded depth and ocean inputs. They do not
   establish exact CPU/GPU agreement across every interpolated mesh attribute,
   arbitrary external shader, water-body blend or far-world CPU phase.
4. No new save format, authority mode, default, infinite boundary, physical
   water source, executor, packet or erosion permission was introduced.
5. This checkpoint's diff was self-reviewed against ownership, lifecycle,
   dedicated-side separation, public overload compatibility and input bounds.
   It has not received an independent reviewer or full-repository test pass.

Remaining implementation includes disturbance stress coverage, generalizing
the bounded physical regions and their verified interfaces, plus actual
disk/restart acceptance of the controlled dam. Continue to use the existing
Data/Tick Engine lanes, canonical transactions, projection safety halo, flood
claims and synchronization.

For manual acceptance in a disposable client world:

1. Inspect an inland lake and coast with built-in water enabled. Compare calm,
   existing storm and freezing conditions while crossing the water surface.
   Confirm anchored dry/unloaded edges, retained base fill/tide, and no new gaps.
2. Make nearby entries and boat wakes. Check that ripple growth is smooth and
   that ordinary wake animation does not continuously dirty chunk meshes.
3. Compare entry/wake effects inside and outside 96 blocks from the local
   player, including moving boats back into range and dimension changes.
   Confirm nearby effects remain available during distant entity activity.
4. Reload resources and test supported external-shader ownership changes.
   Repeat with different graphics presets and two clients. These checks have
   not been performed by the numerical/GPU fixtures.
